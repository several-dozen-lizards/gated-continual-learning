# Evidence reassessment and accepted-fact replay

Frozen before continuation outcomes. Continue the three existing Qwen3.5-2B gated adapters (seeds 17, 29, 43), with fresh optimizers, NF4 weights and rank-16 all-linear LoRA. This is a conditional continuation of the previous cohort, not three new independent replications. Learning rate 0.0002, 20 replays, batch size 4; unchanged training prompts and held-out paraphrases. Evaluation is forced choice among four color tokens, not unrestricted conversation.

The informational bank contains two useful and twelve false claims. Source histories are synthetic audit records, independent of current answers: 13 correct and 3 incorrect observations. A Beta(1,3) prior gives estimated reliability 0.7. Four candidate colors have uniform priors. Each distinct source lineage contributes a categorical likelihood, with error mass divided equally among the other colors. Conflicting votes within one lineage abstain. Unknown sources are neutral. Aliases share a lineage. The decision threshold is 0.9, derived from assumed false-update cost 9 and deferral cost 1; these are explicit design assumptions.

Evidence includes independent corroboration of the two missed useful claims, twelve aliases repeating each of four false claims, independent conflicting reports for four false claims, two apparently independent sources agreeing on one false claim, and one unsupported false claim. Events are shuffled reproducibly. The gate never reads evaluator labels. Source calibration and lineage metadata are supplied, not learned. Agreement can be wrong: coordinated false agreement is expected to pass this gate.

Every event recomputes routes, including possible demotion. Weight updates happen once after the evidence batch using final selections. Demotion does not implement unlearning. CPU controls remove vote content (sham) or incorrectly count aliases as independent. Sham leaves the checkpoint unchanged and shares its baseline evaluation.

Four GPU arms start from exactly the same saved adapter: reassess selected claims; random claims matched by token lengths and whether their subjects already occur in the accepted ledger; reassess plus replay of the merged accepted ledger; random plus the same replay policy. Random selection requires distinct subjects to match ledger size. The ledger starts with previously accepted facts and overwrites by subject, without truth filtering. A wrongly admitted claim can corrupt this ledger. Each pair must match examples, input and supervised tokens, padding, and optimizer steps. Replay arms have greater budgets than non-replay arms; comparisons across these pairs are not equal-cost claims.

Report overall accuracy, retention/correction/new groups, recovery of the two missed useful claims, preservation of the six earlier accepted updates, coordinated-false adoption, and training time and tokens. Separate routing, setup, evaluation, and serialization costs. Wall time is not energy or FLOPs. Show seed variation, not significance claims. Verify prior reload and fresh final adapter reload probabilities, finite losses, frozen input hashes and equal paired budgets. Preserve previous experiment results. No resident integration or external tracking.
